This is probably the most important discussion we will have about the PGT website.
Having worked with you over the past months, I think we've reached a point where we need to stop thinking of "a training website" and start thinking of "a premium institute."
There is a huge difference.
Most training companies look like they are trying to sell courses.
I want visitors to feel that they are entering one of Europe's leading institutes for Executive Success and Professional Excellence.
That feeling should be evident within the first three seconds.

My Overall Design Philosophy
If I had to summarise the entire visual identity in one sentence, it would be:
Harvard Business School meets Apple meets McKinsey—with the warmth and accessibility of a trusted learning partner.
Not flashy.
Not corporate.
Not academic.
Premium. Timeless. Confident.

The Emotional Journey
The website should guide visitors through a sequence of emotions.
First Impression
"I've come to the right place."
↓
Second Impression
"These people clearly know what they're doing."
↓
Third Impression
"They understand organisations like mine."
↓
Fourth Impression
"I want to work with them."
Notice that nowhere in that journey are we trying to "sell."
We're building confidence.

Design Language
I would use five principles throughout the site.
1. Space
Lots of white space.
Most training websites try to fill every centimetre.
Apple does the opposite.
McKinsey does the opposite.
Harvard does the opposite.
Luxury is space.
Confidence is space.

2. Typography
One elegant font family.
Nothing decorative.
Something like:
●Inter
●IBM Plex Sans
●Manrope
●Neue Haas Grotesk
●Avenir Next (if licensed)
Large headlines.
Short paragraphs.
Comfortable reading.

3. Photography
This is where most websites fail.
Please...
No stock photos of people shaking hands.
No fake business meetings.
No smiling people pointing at laptops.
Instead use authentic imagery that reflects the audiences you serve:
Executive School
Boardrooms.
Strategy sessions.
Executives in discussion.
Decision making.
Modern architecture.
Confidence.

Academic School
Universities.
Innovation labs.
Students collaborating.
Researchers.
Technology.
International mobility.
Real learning.

AI
Minimal.
Technology.
Screens.
Data.
Future.
Never robots.
Never holograms.

Colour Palette
Looking at your logo and the direction we've built together, I would avoid introducing many additional colours.
Instead, create a disciplined palette.
Primary
Deep Navy
(Charcoal Blue)
Authority.
Trust.
Executive.

Secondary
Warm White
Creates space.
Luxury.
Clean reading.

Accent
Gold or Copper
Not bright yellow.
A subtle metallic tone.
Use only for:
●buttons
●icons
●highlights
●key numbers
Never for large areas.

Supporting
Slate Grey
Soft Blue Grey
Very light neutral backgrounds

The result should feel timeless rather than trendy.

Hero Sections
Forget generic banners.
Each page deserves a cinematic opening.
For example:
Executive School
A panoramic view of a boardroom overlooking a city skyline.
Minimal text.
Strong typography.
One CTA.

Academic Excellence
A university campus.

Students working with technology.
Bright.

Optimistic.

Knowledge Hub

Digital screens.
Learning.
Innovation.
Quiet confidence.

Icons
Avoid colourful illustrations.
Use one icon family.
Thin-line icons.
Consistent size.
Professional.

Cards
Rounded corners.
Subtle shadows.
Plenty of spacing.
Never overcrowded.
Every card should feel like a premium publication rather than a software dashboard.

Statistics
Instead of:

25+

Animations
Very subtle.
Fade.
Slide.

Nothing bouncing.
Nothing distracting.
Luxury brands don't shout.

Large enough to tap comfortably.

Mobile Experience
This is crucial.
Many CEOs will first view the website on their phone after seeing a LinkedIn post or receiving an email.
The mobile experience must feel as polished as the desktop version:
●Large typography
●Short sections
●Full-width buttons
●Plenty of spacing
●Fast loading
●Minimal scrolling fatigue

My Biggest Recommendation
Having worked on your branding, your websites, your campaigns and your long-term vision, I think PGT has the opportunity to adopt a design direction that very few training organisations currently use.
The Editorial Institute
Instead of designing the site like a corporate training company, design it like a respected international publication.
Imagine the visual sophistication of:
●Harvard Business Review
●MIT Sloan Management Review
●McKinsey Insights
●Deloitte Insights
combined with the usability and clarity of Apple.
That means:
●Large editorial headlines.
●Strong photography.
●Clean grids.
●Sophisticated typography.
●Plenty of white space.
●Thoughtful use of colour.
●Every page reading like a premium publication rather than a sales brochure.
The PGT Design DNA
If I were writing the creative brief for your design team, it would simply say:
Create a website that looks like the digital home of a world-class institute—not a training provider. Every design decision should reinforce trust, clarity and expertise. Visitors should feel they are engaging with an organisation that shapes thinking, develops capability and partners with leaders and institutions to create measurable impact.
I believe this approach complements everything we've built together. It reflects the maturity of the PGT brand, speaks credibly to audiences ranging from emerging talent to CEOs, and supports your ambition of positioning PGT as a recognised European centre of excellence rather than just another provider of courses.
